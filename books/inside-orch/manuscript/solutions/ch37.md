## 3. Cleanup on return is not fencing during partition

A node partitions while its container continues. The control plane eventually marks it lost, removes its active assignment and replaces a stateless workload elsewhere. Until the old node reconnects and consumes cleanup, both physical workloads may exist. Returned-node reconciliation lists the managed unassigned old object, stops/removes it and reports removed. The fake test observes that cleanup sequence; it does not observe exclusive service traffic or storage fencing throughout the partition.

## 4. Match state owner to recovery claim

Reconstructed controllers can recompute from retained records. The combined actual experiment restarted the binary and found services/tasks/nodes absent while an exact manually associated nginx container survived. Durable rollout recovery requires a server actually composing persistent transactional state, fresh-process reads of deployment/task/version records, and observed recovery/cleanup of corresponding engine objects. Capture initial IDs, UTC transitions and final absence. Remove only owned exact objects/databases; never sweep unrelated containers. A healthy endpoint is insufficient recovery evidence.
